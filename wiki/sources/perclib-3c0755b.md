## Scope of the dedicated pointers

Twenty-three rows now point to specific sections in the foundations/models bundle. This is a reconciliation of existing exposition, not a second implementation or tag registry. In particular, the configuration-space proof and fixed-observation conditional kernel were already present there; their absence from the earlier reading column was not a missing mathematical proof.

The pointers distinguish the scope of the formalized module from the larger article. The BK article proceeds beyond the low-density module. The hierarchical article's radial estimates do not identify critical correlations. The tightness article separates target properties from verified special cases. An explicit conditional kernel for a deterministic observed set does not justify an adaptive infinite exploration. These distinctions survive even when a module and article discuss the same notation.

## Remaining checks

For each mathematical interface, identify its exact assumptions and conclusion, then reconcile it with an existing assertion before allocating another tag. Definitions and target propositions must be distinguished from proofs. In particular, the tightness target definitions and their singleton examples do not discharge the general targets; the low-density BK module does not alone discharge the arbitrary-profile result; and a summable hierarchical kernel does not identify its critical two-point function.

The compiled manifest and fingerprint-bound correspondence records remain the authority for displayed proof and review status. No such status is inferred from a module's filename, a source theorem keyword, or a link in this table. Missing associations remain coverage obligations even when the corresponding source proof is already present.

The source cards retain the relevant limits of each correspondence:

- Deterministic walk kernels allow arbitrary vertex sets, infinite weights and unrestricted diagonal entries. Their bond application removes the diagonal before taking row sums. Intensity bounds keep finite parameters, possibly infinite thresholds and the zero off-diagonal row case, which does not force diagonal intensities to vanish.
- Finite restrictions and fixed disjoint blocks allow arbitrary ambient index sets. The joint ordered-coupling statement retains countability; its nonnegative expectation comparison includes infinite values.
- Local connectivity requires only a countable domain for measurability, even in an uncountable ambient model. The finite-domain restriction law is an additional cylinder argument. Probability limits are stated for measurable events; arbitrary-domain formal measure expressions are not silently given that interpretation.
- A finite observation window need not give a finite-coordinate event. Finite witnesses and increasing finite-edge approximations do not justify differentiation of the infinite-volume probability.
- Cylinder Russo uses signed forced differences without increasingness. Pivotal conversion requires increasingness. The ordinary derivative-limit interpretation assumes accumulation of the parameter domain, unlike the formal within-derivative predicate at isolated points.
- Boundary wiring uses independent disjoint coordinate blocks. General injective sampling is valid for arbitrary index sets; closed-block products require measurable countable coordinate events. The initial ray segments attain the first-exit boundary bound exactly.
- Full configuration containment in a simple host forces diagonal probabilities to zero. Graph containment does not. The graph-only criterion and converse directions are explicit expository consequences beyond the inspected host interface.
- Cluster measurability uses countability, while the deterministic extended indicator sum and path identities do not. Forest formulas retain empty paths, extended susceptibility and both endpoint parameters on the ray.
