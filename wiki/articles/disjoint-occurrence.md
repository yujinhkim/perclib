## Certificates {#section-certificates}

<a id="tag-OQN6" data-assertion="definition" data-title="Finite forcing certificates and disjoint occurrence"></a><p>A finite set $F\subseteq I$ is a certificate for an event $A$ at $\omega$ if every configuration agreeing with $\omega$ on $F$ belongs to $A$. Thus a certificate specifies both the open and the closed coordinates of $\omega$ in $F$, and must force $A$ regardless of all other coordinates. The <em>disjoint occurrence</em> $A\circ B$ consists of configurations admitting disjoint finite certificates for $A$ and $B.</p><p>For bond percolation, the coordinates are edges. The certificates must be edge-disjoint; they may meet at vertices.</p>

<a id="tag-X7ET" data-assertion="proposition" data-title="Containment, symmetry and monotonicity of disjoint occurrence"></a><p>For arbitrary events,</p><div class="math-display">$$A\circ B\subseteq A\cap B,\qquad A\circ B=B\circ A.$$</div><p>If $A\subseteq C$ and $B\subseteq D$, then $A\circ B\subseteq C\circ D$.</p><p><em>Proof.</em> A configuration agrees with itself on each certificate, so both events occur. Swapping the certificates proves symmetry. A certificate forcing an event also forces every larger event, which proves monotonicity.</p>

<p>The empty event cannot occur disjointly with any event, by containment in their intersection. Two copies of the sure event occur disjointly everywhere, using two empty certificates; this includes an empty coordinate set. The universal forcing condition can also be considered for an infinite set $F$: a configuration forcing $A$ on $F$ itself belongs to $A$, as does $F\cap\omega$, since both agree with $\omega$ on $F$. Only the definition of disjoint occurrence requires its forcing sets to be finite.</p>

## Increasing events {#section-open-witnesses}

<a id="tag-HRBD" data-assertion="proposition" data-title="Open-witness characterization and increasingness"></a><p>If $A$ and $B$ are increasing, then $\omega\in A\circ B$ precisely when there are finite disjoint sets $F,H\subseteq\omega$ such that $F\in A$ and $H\in B$, where $F$ and $H$ are regarded as configurations with every other coordinate closed. In particular, $A\circ B$ is increasing.</p><p><em>Proof.</em> Given certificates $F,H$ at $\omega$, the configuration $F\cap\omega$ agrees with $\omega$ on $F$, so it belongs to $A$; similarly $H\cap\omega\in B$. These two open sets are finite and disjoint. Conversely, if $F\subseteq\omega$ and $F\in A$, every configuration agreeing with $\omega$ on $F$ contains $F$, hence belongs to $A$ by increasingness. The same argument applies to $H$. Finally, the same open witnesses work in every configuration containing $\omega$.</p><p>For the single-coordinate event $A_i=\{\omega:i\in\omega\}$, every open witness contains $i$. Consequently $A_i\circ A_i=\varnothing$, whereas $A_i\circ A_j=A_i\cap A_j$ for $i\ne j$, witnessed by $\{i\}$ and $\{j\}$. Disjoint occurrence is a condition on witnesses, not independence of the two events.</p><p>Finiteness matters in infinite volume. If $I$ is infinite and $A=\{I\}$ is the event that every coordinate is open, no finite set certifies $A$: one can close a coordinate outside it. Thus $A\circ\Omega=\varnothing$ even though $A$ is nonempty. On a finite coordinate set, by contrast, $A\circ\Omega=A$ for every event $A$, using certificates $I$ and $\varnothing$.</p>

## Unions and repeated occurrence {#section-algebra}

<a id="tag-17DW" data-assertion="proposition" data-title="Distribution over unions of increasing events"></a><p>For increasing events $A,B,C$,</p><div class="math-display">$$(A\cup B)\circ C=(A\circ C)\cup(B\circ C),\qquad A\circ(B\cup C)=(A\circ B)\cup(A\circ C).$$</div><p><em>Proof.</em> An open witness $F$ belonging to $A\cup B$ belongs to one of $A$ and $B$, so its disjoint companion for $C$ witnesses one of the events on the right. The reverse inclusion follows by monotonicity. Symmetry gives the second identity.</p>

<a id="tag-HP3X" data-assertion="proposition" data-title="Triple open witnesses and associativity"></a><p>For increasing events $A,B,C$, membership in $(A\circ B)\circ C$ is equivalent to the existence of three pairwise disjoint finite sets $F,H,K\subseteq\omega$ with $F\in A$, $H\in B$, and $K\in C$. Consequently</p><div class="math-display">$$(A\circ B)\circ C=A\circ(B\circ C).$$</div><p><em>Proof.</em> Suppose $S$ and $K$ are disjoint open witnesses for $A\circ B$ and $C$. Applying the open-witness characterization inside the configuration $S$ gives disjoint $F,H\subseteq S$ belonging to $A,B$. They are each disjoint from $K$. Conversely, three such witnesses yield the outer witnesses $F\cup H$ and $K$: the former belongs to $A\circ B$ by its witnesses $F,H$. Grouping $H\cup K$ instead gives the same three-witness characterization for $A\circ(B\circ C)$.</p><p>Thus three increasing events occurring disjointly require three pairwise edge-disjoint open witnesses, not three pairwise vertex-disjoint subgraphs. Neither a probability law nor countability is used in these identities.</p>

<p>For three distinct coordinates, the event that all three are open is their triple disjoint occurrence, using three singleton witnesses. If two of the three required coordinates coincide, their two-event disjoint occurrence is empty, so grouping it with any third event still gives the empty event.</p>

## Restricting coordinates {#section-restriction}

<a id="tag-VMVX" data-assertion="proposition" data-title="Disjoint occurrence commutes with coordinate restriction"></a><p>Let $S\subseteq I$ and let $\rho_S(\omega)=\omega\cap S$, viewed as a configuration on $S$. For arbitrary events $A,B\subseteq\{0,1\}^S$,</p><div class="math-display">$$\rho_S^{-1}(A\circ B)=\rho_S^{-1}(A)\circ\rho_S^{-1}(B).$$</div><p>Neither $S$ nor $I$ needs to be finite or countable, and the events need not be increasing.</p><p><em>Proof.</em> Certificates on $S$ also force the corresponding inverse-image events on $I$. Conversely, suppose $F$ certifies $\rho_S^{-1}(A)$ at $\omega$. To see that $F\cap S$ certifies $A$ at $\rho_S(\omega)$, take a configuration $\eta$ on $S$ agreeing there, and extend it to $I$ by retaining $\omega$ outside $S$. The extension agrees with $\omega$ on all of $F$, so its restriction $\eta$ belongs to $A$. Restrict the second certificate in the same way. Intersecting with $S$ preserves finiteness and disjointness.</p><p>In particular, when both events depend on one finite set of coordinates, their disjoint occurrence can be computed entirely on that finite space. This reduction does not identify arbitrary infinite-volume events with events having finite certificates.</p>

## Deterministic product laws {#section-endpoints}

<a id="tag-E7HI" data-assertion="proposition" data-title="Disjoint-occurrence bound for finite deterministic profiles"></a><p>Suppose $I$ is finite and $q_i\in\{0,1\}$ for every coordinate. Then for arbitrary events $A,B$,</p><div class="math-display">$$\mathbb P_q(A\circ B)\leq\mathbb P_q(A)\mathbb P_q(B).$$</div><p><em>Proof.</em> Put $u=\{i:q_i=1\}$. The product law is concentrated at $u$, so every event $C$ has probability $\mathbf1_C(u)$. The bound is therefore the indicator inequality $\mathbf1_{A\circ B}(u)\leq\mathbf1_A(u)\mathbf1_B(u)$, which follows from $A\circ B\subseteq A\cap B$.</p><p>This includes the homogeneous endpoints $p=0$ and $p=1$, as well as mixed zero-one profiles. The BK inequality for increasing events with nondeterministic coordinates is a separate theorem; the deterministic argument above does not prove it. The <a href="https://github.com/tomhutchcroft/lrp/blob/7a303901b9e4708259bf74ca2333c0515a784af8/sources/notes/book_draft.tex#L1223">notes introduce open witnesses and state finite BK immediately after Harris</a>. Their discussion also considers broader infinite-volume witness conventions. Here $\circ$ always means disjoint <em>finite</em> certificates.</p>

<a id="tag-E7K6" data-assertion="proposition" data-title="Deterministic Bernoulli profiles on arbitrary coordinate sets"></a>
<p>For any coordinate set $I$ and any $u\subseteq I$, the product law with $q_i=\mathbf1_{\{i\in u\}}$ equals the Dirac law $\delta_u$ on the product sigma-algebra. Consequently every measurable event $A$ has probability $\mathbf1_A(u)$, and every integrable real observable $f$ has expectation $f(u)$. On a finite coordinate set every event and real observable is measurable and integrable; in particular the all-closed and all-open expectations are $f(\varnothing)$ and $f(I)$.</p>
<p><em>Proof.</em> On each finite-coordinate cylinder the product probability is one if its prescribed coordinates agree with $u$, and zero otherwise. This is exactly its Dirac probability. Finite cylinders form a generating intersection-stable family, so uniqueness of probability measures gives equality on the product sigma-algebra. The event and integral formulas follow.</p>
<p>No countability of $I$ is needed. The equality is an equality of measures on the product sigma-algebra; it does not assert that the singleton event $\{u\}$ is measurable when $I$ is uncountable. In finite volume the indicator formulas immediately give E7HI for arbitrary, possibly decreasing events. With no coordinates, the unique configuration is both empty and all-open.</p>
