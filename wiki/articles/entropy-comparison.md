## Entropy of discrete laws {#section-entropy}

<a id="tag-UVKE" data-assertion="definition" data-title="Relative entropy for countable probability laws"></a><p>For probability laws $\mu,\nu$ on a countable set $S$, their relative entropy is</p><div class="math-display">$$D(\mu\|\nu)=\sum_{s\in S}\mu(s)\log\frac{\mu(s)}{\nu(s)}.$$</div><p>A term with $\mu(s)=0$ is zero; a term with $\mu(s)&gt;0=\nu(s)$ is $+\infty$. The sum is well-defined in $[0,\infty]$. Indeed, its negative part is summable since $t\log t\geq-1/e$, applied with $t=\mu(s)/\nu(s)$, and nonnegativity follows from the log-sum inequality. All logarithms are natural. For Bernoulli laws write</p><div class="math-display">$$d(a\|b)=a\log\frac ab+(1-a)\log\frac{1-a}{1-b},\qquad a,b\in[0,1],$$</div><p>with the same conventions.</p>

<a id="tag-JR0J" data-assertion="proposition" data-title="Discrete entropy chain rule and data processing"></a><p>Let $\mu,\nu$ be laws on a product of two countable sets. If $\mu_X\ll\nu_X$, then</p><div class="math-display">$$D(\mu\|\nu)=D(\mu_X\|\nu_X)+\sum_{x:\,\mu_X(x)&gt;0}\mu_X(x)D\bigl(\mu(\cdot\mid x)\|\nu(\cdot\mid x)\bigr).$$</div><p>If $\mu_X\not\ll\nu_X$, the joint entropy is infinite. For every map $g$ between countable sets,</p><div class="math-display">$$D(g_*\mu\|g_*\nu)\leq D(\mu\|\nu).$$</div><p><em>Proof.</em> On a positive-probability fibre, factor the likelihood ratio as the marginal ratio times the conditional ratio and sum its logarithm. The negative parts of the joint and marginal entropy sums are summable, so this regrouping is valid even when the answer is infinite. The conditional entropies are nonnegative. Applying the identity to $(g(s),s)$ gives data processing. The log-sum inequality used here follows from convexity of $t\log t$: for nonnegative masses $(a_i),(b_i)$,</p><div class="math-display">$$\sum_i a_i\log\frac{a_i}{b_i}\geq\left(\sum_i a_i\right)\log\frac{\sum_i a_i}{\sum_i b_i}.$$</div><p>The zero-mass cases use the stated conventions; the countable version follows by grouping the tail and passing to the limit.</p>

## Comparing an event {#section-event}

<a id="tag-58HC" data-assertion="proposition" data-title="Event-level entropy comparison"></a><p>For countable probability laws $\mu,\nu$ and any event $A$, set $a=\mu(A)$ and $b=\nu(A)$. Then</p><div class="math-display">$$d(a\|b)\leq D(\mu\|\nu),\qquad |a-b|^2\leq2\max\{a,b\}\,d(a\|b).$$</div><p>In particular $|\mu(A)-\nu(A)|^2\leq2\max\{\mu(A),\nu(A)\}D(\mu\|\nu)$ whenever the entropy is finite. If $a=b=0$, the comparison is read as the trivial equality $0=0$, without multiplying zero by infinite entropy.</p><p><em>Proof.</em> Map the sample to its event indicator for the first inequality. For $0&lt;b&lt;1$, the function $u\mapsto d(u\|b)$ vanishes with zero derivative at $u=b$ and has second derivative $1/[u(1-u)]$. Between $a$ and $b$ this is at least $1/\max\{a,b\}$. Taylor's integral formula therefore gives the second inequality. Endpoint values follow by continuity when finite; if $b$ is zero or one and $a\ne b$, the divergence is infinite.</p>

<a id="tag-KHB7" data-assertion="proposition" data-title="Weighted Bernoulli entropy bound"></a><p>For $J\in[0,\infty)$ and $\beta_1,\beta_2\in(0,\infty)$,</p><div class="math-display">$$d(1-e^{-\beta_1J}\|1-e^{-\beta_2J})\leq J\left(\beta_1\log\frac{\beta_1}{\beta_2}-\beta_1+\beta_2\right)\leq\frac{J(\beta_1-\beta_2)^2}{2\min\{\beta_1,\beta_2\}}.$$</div><p><em>Proof.</em> For $J&gt;0$, a Poisson variable of mean $\beta_iJ$ is positive with probability $1-e^{-\beta_iJ}$. Its likelihood ratio against the other Poisson law has logarithm $J(\beta_2-\beta_1)+k\log(\beta_1/\beta_2)$. Taking expectation and applying data processing proves the first bound. As a function of $u$, $u\log(u/v)-u+v$ vanishes with zero derivative at $u=v$ and has second derivative $1/u$. Taylor's integral formula bounds it by $(u-v)^2/[2\min\{u,v\}]$. When $J=0$, both Bernoulli laws are the point mass at zero.</p>

## The cost of a finite transcript {#section-finite}

<p>Run a deterministic tree on coordinates indexed by $I$. At each step the tree either halts or chooses an unqueried coordinate as a function of the preceding answers. After halting, append a fixed cemetery symbol forever. Let $X^m$ be the first $m$ answers; the chosen coordinates can be recovered from this string and the rule. Write $\operatorname{Rev}_q^m(T,i)$ for the probability under profile $q$ that coordinate $i$ is queried within these steps.</p>

<a id="tag-CH4N" data-assertion="proposition" data-title="Finite transcript entropy equals its revealment cost"></a><p>Suppose $q_i,r_i\in(0,1)$ for every coordinate the tree may query. Then</p><div class="math-display">$$D\bigl(\mathcal L_q(X^m)\|\mathcal L_r(X^m)\bigr)=\sum_i\operatorname{Rev}_q^m(T,i)\,d(q_i\|r_i).$$</div><p>The same identity holds if some coordinates satisfy $q_i=r_i\in\{0,1\}$, assigning them cost zero. Although $I$ can be arbitrary, only finitely many coordinates occur in a depth-$m$ tree.</p><p><em>Proof.</em> Every reachable prefix has positive probability under both laws. Conditional on it, the next unqueried bit has its original Bernoulli law by the <a href="/tag/56TE/">conditional transcript formula</a>. The rule chooses the same coordinate under the two conditional laws; a halted prefix contributes zero. Iterating the chain rule thus gives the expectation, under $q$, of the sum of costs along the run. Since no coordinate repeats, the expected number of queries at $i$ is exactly its revealment probability.</p><p>The asymmetry matters: $D(\mathcal L_q\|\mathcal L_r)$ averages the cost under $q$. Neither the queried set nor its size is independent of the answers.</p>

## Countable decision trees {#section-countable}

<a id="tag-D66F" data-assertion="theorem" data-title="Weighted entropy comparison for Borel-computing decision trees"></a><p>Let $I$ be countable, give each coordinate a finite weight $J_i\geq0$, and let $\mathbb P_\beta$ have independent open probabilities $1-e^{-\beta J_i}$. Let $T$ be a deterministic, non-repeating decision tree, allowed to run forever. Suppose a Borel event $A$ is a Borel function of its full answer transcript: there is a Borel set $B\subseteq\{0,1,\dagger\}^{\mathbb N}$ such that $\mathbf1_A(\omega)=\mathbf1_B(X(\omega))$ for every configuration. Put</p><div class="math-display">$$C_{\beta_1}(T)=\sum_{i\in I}J_i\operatorname{Rev}_{\beta_1}(T,i).$$</div><p>For $\beta_1,\beta_2\in(0,\infty)$ with $C_{\beta_1}(T)&lt;\infty$,</p><div class="math-display">$$|\mathbb P_{\beta_1}(A)-\mathbb P_{\beta_2}(A)|^2\leq\frac{(\beta_1-\beta_2)^2}{\min\{\beta_1,\beta_2\}}\max\{\mathbb P_{\beta_1}(A),\mathbb P_{\beta_2}(A)\}\,C_{\beta_1}(T).$$</div><p>When the cost is infinite, the comparison gives no finite bound; equal intensities or two zero event probabilities are trivial cases stated separately.</p><p><em>Proof.</em> The finite-transcript identity and one-coordinate estimate give, for every $m$,</p><div class="math-display">$$D\bigl(\mathcal L_{\beta_1}(X^m)\|\mathcal L_{\beta_2}(X^m)\bigr)\leq\frac{(\beta_1-\beta_2)^2}{2\min\{\beta_1,\beta_2\}}C_{\beta_1}(T).$$</div><p>Apply the event comparison to any event determined by $X^m$. To pass to $B$, let $\lambda$ be the average of the two full transcript laws. Finite-prefix cylinder sets form an algebra generating the Borel sigma-algebra of the transcript space, so there are finite-prefix events $B_k$ with $\lambda(B_k\triangle B)\to0$. For completeness, the sets approximable in measure by this algebra form a sigma-algebra: complements preserve the error, finite unions add errors, and a countable union is first approximated by a sufficiently large finite union. Thus the approximation applies to every Borel $B$. Both transcript laws assign $B_k$ probabilities converging to their probabilities of $B$. The same bound holds for each $B_k$, independently of its prefix length, and passage to the limit proves the result.</p><p>This argument neither assumes that the tree halts nor conditions on a completed infinite transcript. The common Borel output rule is essential to the proof; separate completed sigma-algebras under the two laws do not supply such a rule. Reversing the two intensities gives a second bound with revealment cost under $\beta_2$.</p>

<p>The <a href="https://github.com/tomhutchcroft/lrp/blob/7a303901b9e4708259bf74ca2333c0515a784af8/sources/notes/book_draft.tex#L2134">relative-entropy and decision-tree sections of the notes</a> develop this comparison, with the weighted theorem stated at <a href="https://github.com/tomhutchcroft/lrp/blob/7a303901b9e4708259bf74ca2333c0515a784af8/sources/notes/book_draft.tex#L2189">S013</a>. Here positive intensities, finite individual weights, and the finite-cost case are explicit. The proof passes through finite-prefix event approximation rather than introducing entropy for infinite transcript laws. Applying the result to a cluster exploration additionally requires constructing a tree that computes the event and bounding its weighted revealments; those steps are supplied in the <a href="#/stopped-exploration">stopped-exploration article</a>.</p>

## A finite covariance inequality {#section-osss}

<a id="tag-29WG" data-assertion="theorem" data-title="Finite OSSS covariance inequality with an increasing comparator"></a>
<p>Let $\omega=(\omega_i)_{i\in I}$ have independent Bernoulli coordinates with probabilities $p_i\in[0,1]$, where $I$ is finite. Let $f:\{0,1\}^I\to[0,1]$ and let $g:\{0,1\}^I\to\mathbb R$ be increasing. If a deterministic non-repeating decision tree computes $f$ on every configuration, and $\delta_i$ is its probability of querying coordinate $i$, then</p>
<div class="math-display">$$|\operatorname{Cov}(f,g)|\leq\sum_{i\in I}\delta_i\operatorname{Cov}(g,\omega_i).$$</div>
<p>No monotonicity of $f$ is required. The increasing hypothesis on $g$ makes every covariance on the right nonnegative.</p>
<p><em>Proof.</em> Run the tree on $\omega$, and take an independent copy $\omega'$. After the first $t$ queries, form a hybrid $\omega^t$ by replacing the queried coordinates of $\omega$ with the corresponding coordinates of $\omega'$. Thus $\omega^0=\omega$. Conditional on the completed query transcript, the unqueried original coordinates retain their product law, and the replacements are independent with the same marginals. The terminal hybrid therefore has the original product law independently of the transcript. Since the transcript determines $f(\omega)$, telescoping gives</p>
<div class="math-display">$$\operatorname{Cov}(f,g)=\sum_t\mathbb E\!\left[f(\omega)\bigl(g(\omega^{t-1})-g(\omega^t)\bigr)\right],$$</div>
<p>where increments after halting are zero.</p>
<p>Fix a possible query prefix just before coordinate $i$ is queried. Condition further on all original coordinates except $i$ and on the replacement values already used. Averaging over $\omega_i$ and $\omega'_i$ makes this increment</p>
<div class="math-display">$$p_i(1-p_i)\,\Delta_i f(\omega)\,\Delta_i g(\omega^{t-1}),$$</div>
<p>where each difference means the value with coordinate $i$ set to one minus the value with it set to zero. Since $|\Delta_i f|\leq1$ and $\Delta_i g\geq0$, its absolute value is at most $p_i(1-p_i)\Delta_i g(\omega^{t-1})$. Given the prefix, the hybrid coordinates other than $i$ have their original product marginals independently: previously queried values have been replaced, and the remaining original coordinates have not been conditioned on. Averaging the bound therefore gives</p>
<div class="math-display">$$p_i(1-p_i)\mathbb E[\Delta_i g]=\operatorname{Cov}(g,\omega_i).$$</div>
<p>Sum over the prefixes and query times. Because no coordinate is queried twice, their probabilities sum to $\delta_i$. The triangle inequality proves the result. The same calculation includes $p_i=0$ or $1$, when its contribution is zero.</p>
<p>This supplies a finite version of the notes' S017 with the monotonicity condition made explicit. Without that condition the assertion is false: for one fair bit, $f=g=1-\omega$ has covariance $1/4$ but $\operatorname{Cov}(g,\omega)=-1/4$. Infinite-volume applications below use finite graphs and a limiting argument, not an unproved infinite-tree covariance formula.</p>
